1. Experimental Details

Materials and synthesis. Co(NO₃)₂·6H₂O, Zn(NO₃)₂·6H₂O, Fe(NO₃)₃·9H₂O, Mn(acac)₃, 2-methylimidazole, and methanol were analytical-grade reagents from Aladdin. Analytical-grade ethanol and KOH were supplied by Tianjin Fengchuan and Sinopharm, respectively. In addition, 5 wt% Nafion solution was from Sigma-Aldrich, and 20 wt% Pt/C from Johnson Matthey. Deionized water was used throughout, and reagents were used as received. The FeCoMn–NC recipe used 208, 856, 289, and 252 mg of the Co, Zn, Fe, and Mn precursors, respectively, in 50 mL methanol, and 2.35 g 2-methylimidazole in another 50 mL methanol was added dropwise. The mixture was stirred at room temperature for 24 h. The solid was collected by centrifugation, washed with ethanol, and vacuum-dried at 60 °C. The precursor was heated in a ceramic boat at 5 °C min⁻¹ to 1000 °C under Ar/H₂ (95:5) and held for 2 h. FeCo–NC omitted the Mn source, and Mn–NC omitted the Fe and Co sources. Products were not acid-washed.

Electrochemical measurements. Electrochemical measurements used Gamry Interface 1000/1010E potentiostats and a three-electrode cell containing a Pine E7R9 rotating ring-disk working electrode (5.61 mm glassy-carbon disk and Pt ring), a saturated calomel reference electrode (SCE), and a graphite-rod counter electrode. The electrolyte was O₂-saturated 0.1 M KOH.

Electrode preparation and potential scale. The catalyst ink contained 3 mg catalyst dispersed in 150 μL deionized water, 50 μL ethanol, and 10 μL 5 wt% Nafion solution (210 μL in total) and was sonicated for 30 min. An 18 μL aliquot was drop-cast onto the glassy-carbon disk and dried at room temperature, giving 0.257 mg catalyst per disk, i.e., 1.04 mg cm⁻² for the geometric disk area of 0.2472 cm². All current densities were obtained by dividing the recorded currents by this geometric area. Potentials measured against the SCE were converted to the RHE scale by E(RHE) = E(SCE) + 0.241 + 0.059 × pH, which gives E(RHE) = E(SCE) + 1.016 V for the electrolyte used. iR compensation was applied by the potentiostat during acquisition, and no further ohmic correction was applied during data processing. The fixed +1.016 V conversion is used throughout.

LSVs were recorded at 5 mV s⁻¹ at rotation rates of 400, 625, 900, 1225, 1600, 2025, and 2500 rpm.

Electrochemical definitions. E_(onset) is the potential interpolated at a baseline-corrected current density of −0.10 mA cm⁻². E_(1/2) is the potential at half of j_(L), where j_(L) is the median current density between 0.30 and 0.40 V versus RHE. The baseline is the median between 1.00 and 1.05 V. The kinetic-current magnitude is |j·j_(L)/(j_(L) − j)|, evaluated using the mean j within ±5 mV of the stated potential. Tafel slopes were obtained by regression of E against log₁₀|j_(k)|. The fitted segments shown in Figure 3c are 0.860–0.907, 0.860–0.908, 0.863–0.911, and 0.879–0.928 V for FeCoMn–NC, FeCo–NC, Mn–NC, and Pt/C, respectively. Display smoothing was not used to calculate E_(1/2) or j_(k).

RRDE acquisition and statistics. FeCoMn–NC and Pt/C were recorded at 1600 rpm with disk scan rates of 7 and 10 mV s⁻¹, respectively. Their ring potentials were 0.300 and 0.389 V versus SCE (1.316 and 1.405 V versus RHE). The disk and ring current magnitudes were used directly, with nominal N = 0.37. Reported n and H₂O₂ values are averages over points within 0.30–0.75 V.

Cyclic voltammetry and chronoamperometry. CVs were recorded on a stationary electrode at 10–50 mV s⁻¹ in N₂- or O₂-saturated 0.1 M KOH (Figure S8). Chronoamperometry was performed at 0.516 V versus RHE and 1600 rpm. Relative current is I(t)/I_(ref) × 100%, with I_(ref) taken at the first point at or after 0.1 h to exclude the initial charging transient. The long-term stability test lasted 238 h. This test was accumulated from three consecutive segments on the same electrode (111.1, 111.1, and 16.2 h). Retention was evaluated from the final 120 recorded points relative to the same initial reference. The shaded envelope in Figure 3g is the rolling minimum–maximum of the current trace.

Characterization. Morphology was examined by field-emission SEM (Hitachi S-4800) and TEM/HRTEM with EDS mapping (JEOL JEM-2100F). Samples for TEM were ultrasonically dispersed in ethanol and drop-cast onto carbon-coated copper grids. XRD patterns were collected using a Bruker D8 Advance diffractometer with Cu Kα radiation (λ = 0.15406 nm). XPS measurements used a Kratos Axis Supra spectrometer with Al Kα radiation (1486.6 eV). Binding energies were referenced to C 1s at 284.8 eV, and component fitting used a Shirley background.

Fe, Co, and Mn K-edge XAS spectra of the samples were acquired on a laboratory high-throughput X-ray absorption fine structure spectrometer (Anhui Absorption Spectroscopy Instrument Co., Ltd., China) at the Tianjin University large-scale instrument platform in transmission geometry, with the corresponding metal foils measured in the same session for energy calibration. Larch²⁵ was used for background removal, Fourier transformation, and fitting. The background radius R_(bkg) was 1.0 Å (1.3 Å for the MnO reference). The k²-weighted data were transformed with a Hanning window (dk = 1 Å⁻¹). Fe and Co fits used k = 2.5–11.5 Å⁻¹ and R = 1.0–3.2 Å, and Mn fits used k = 2.0–6.0 Å⁻¹ and R = 1.0–3.0 Å. The FT spectra are not phase-corrected. Morlet wavelet transforms used η = 10 and σ = 1.²⁶

XAS processing and fitting. The main plotted sample uses the Fe2, Co2, and Mn2 acquisition files. Background extraction used the recorded nominal edge energies Fe 7112, Co 7709, and Mn 6539 eV, while foil-derivative maxima defined the reference-energy alignment. Hanning windows with dk = 1 Å⁻¹ and k² weighting were used. Fe and Co two-shell fits used total metal-scattering paths with a shared occupancy factor for the bcc first and second shells, and the corresponding coordination degeneracies are 8 and 6. Foil-calibrated amplitude factors S₀² were 0.77 for Fe and 0.78 for Co. Mn fitting used S₀² = 0.8 and σ² = 0.010 Å² for the reported light-atom shell.

Image and spectral processing. SEM/TEM panels were cropped from the raw instrument files (tif/dm4/bmp) at final print size. TEM contrast was linearly stretched, and no filtering or denoising was applied. Lattice-fringe analysis (Figure S2) used a 512 × 512 px window (0.01382 nm px⁻¹), a Hann-windowed FFT, and a grey-level profile averaged over 41 lines normal to the fringes (σ = 1.5 px Gaussian smoothing for peak finding). XRD reference sticks for bcc FeCo were calculated from Bragg’s law with a = 2.857 Å and Cu Kα radiation. XPS components (Table S1) were fitted after Shirley background subtraction. All electrochemical metrics in Table S3 were recomputed from the raw potentiostat files with a single script using the definitions given above. Curve smoothing in the figures is for display only.

ATR-SEIRAS acquisition and processing. The FTIR instrument was a Thermo Fisher Nicolet iS50 with an Au-film-coated Si prism. Catalyst ink was drop-cast onto the Au film. The background spectrum was collected at the open-circuit potential with the catalyst film in place, so the potential-independent absorption of the film and Nafion is removed from all spectra. Spectra were then measured in O₂-saturated 0.1 M KOH as the potential was stepped from approximately 1.2 to 0 V versus RHE. A piecewise linear baseline anchored at 1330–1370, 1900–1950, 2550–2650, and 3850–3950 cm⁻¹ was subtracted from each spectrum, and difference spectra were referenced to 1.2 V. Savitzky–Golay smoothing (second order) over approximately 10 or 20 cm⁻¹ was applied for display only. The region below 1000 cm⁻¹ is not shown because it is dominated by absorption of the Si prism. Si–O absorption of the Si prism was excluded as the origin of the band near 1215 cm⁻¹ on the basis of the control measurement reported by Sun et al. (Nat. Catal. 2023, 6, 1164–1173)

2. Computational Details

2.1 Computational platforms, models and atom constraints

Calculations were carried out on the supercomputing system of the Institute for Materials Research (IMR), Tohoku University, Japan, and Genkai at Kyushu University, Japan. VASP 6.6.0 with PAW-PBE and D3(BJ) dispersion was used. Reference numbers in this section follow the main-text bibliography. Relaxation energies and higher-precision electronic-analysis energies are kept separate.

Models and structural constraints. The supported models use the same orthorhombic 22.198 × 21.360 × 24.000 Å³ cell and a Γ-centered 1 × 1 × 1 k-point mesh. Their clean compositions are C₁₇₄N₄Fe₂₇Co₃₂Mn, C₁₈₀Fe₂₇Co₃₂, and C₁₇₄N₄Mn. Each particle-containing model fixes 15 interior particle atoms, and all atoms of Mn–NC are free. The relaxed surface states under reaction conditions (8O* + Mn–OH* for FeCoMn–NC, 8O* for FeCo–NC, and Mn=O for Mn–NC) contain 248, 247, and 180 atoms, respectively. Their c-axis empty spans are approximately 12.59, 12.62, and 21.81 Å, calculated as the cell height minus the atomic z span. The fixed atoms (1-based indices) are 183, 189, 191, 192, 193, 195, 201, 213, 214, 217, 218, 225, 226, 229 and 230 in FeCoMn–NC and 185, 191, 193, 194, 195, 197, 203, 215, 216, 219, 220, 227, 228, 231 and 232 in FeCo–NC. They carry the fixed selective-dynamics flags in the deposited structure files (Data Availability Statement in the main text). The 59-atom particle represents a local particle–support environment.

The independent FeCo(110) model contains 16 Fe and 16 Co atoms in a four-layer slab. Its orthorhombic cell is 8.081 × 5.714 × 21.061 Å³, and the clean slab fixes 16 atoms in the lower two layers while the upper 16 metal atoms and adsorbates can relax. Its Γ-centered k-point mesh is 5 × 4 × 1.

2.2 Calculation settings

Geometry optimization. PAW-PBE calculations used a 500 eV cutoff, D3(BJ) dispersion (IVDW = 12), ISPIN = 2, PREC = Normal, ALGO = Fast, EDIFF = 10⁻⁵ eV, EDIFFG = −0.02 eV Å⁻¹, IBRION = 2, POTIM = 0.1, ISIF = 2, ISYM = 0, and LREAL = Auto. Gaussian smearing used SIGMA = 0.05 eV. The standard PAW datasets were C, N, Fe, Co, Mn_(pv), O, and H as required by composition, and Mn semicore p states were included. Initial moments were Fe/Mn 3, Co 2, O 0.5, and C/N/H 0 μB. Spin mixing used AMIX/BMIX = 0.1/0.0001 and AMIX_MAG/BMIX_MAG = 0.4/0.0001 with LMAXMIX = 4. A structure was accepted from the ionic-convergence marker and normal termination, and the final σ → 0 energy was used.

Supported-model optimizations used NELM = 150, NSW = 800 for the ORR runs and up to 400 steps in the binding/recovery runs. The latter retain their own initial moments. In particular, the FeCoMn–NC recovery input initialized Mn at 4 μB, whereas the ORR template initialized it at 3 μB. No spin constraint was imposed. The optimizations started from ISTART = 0 and ICHARG = 2, used the automatic fine FFT grid, and wrote LWAVE and LCHARG output.

Electronic-structure and charge analysis. Fixed-geometry calculations used ENCUT = 500 eV, EDIFF = 10⁻⁶ eV, PREC = Accurate, LREAL = False, ALGO = Normal, ISMEAR = 0, SIGMA = 0.05 eV, IVDW = 12, LMAXMIX = 4, LORBIT = 11, NEDOS = 2000, LAECHG = True, and Γ-point sampling. The realized fine charge-density grid was 252 × 240 × 360.

The static calculations used NSW = 0, IBRION = −1, NELM = 200, ISYM = 0, ISTART = 0 and ICHARG = 2. LVHAR, LWAVE and LCHARG are True, and the spin-mixing parameters are the same as in the optimizations. All fragments are evaluated at the coordinates of their corresponding full state.

FeCo(110) model. The adsorption calculations used ENCUT = 500 eV, PREC = Normal, EDIFF = 10⁻⁵ eV, EDIFFG = −0.02 eV Å⁻¹, NSW = 300, IBRION = 2, POTIM = 0.2, ISIF = 2, ISYM = 0, LREAL = False, ALGO = Normal, NELM = 200, ISMEAR = 0, SIGMA = 0.10 eV, ISPIN = 2, LMAXMIX = 4 and IVDW = 12. Initial Fe/Co/O moments were 3/2/0.5 μB, and hydrogen initial moments were zero. LDIPOL = True and IDIPOL = 3 apply the dipole correction normal to the slab. DIPOL follows the slab/adsorbate center used in each input (z = 0.5310 for 6O and 0.5392 for 8O in fractional coordinates). Spin-mixing parameters were AMIX = 0.1, BMIX = 0.0001, AMIX_MAG = 0.4 and BMIX_MAG = 0.0001. LWAVE and LCHARG were False.

2.3 Thermochemical conventions and electrochemical potential

E denotes the final converged VASP energy (σ → 0). Gas references G(H₂) and G(H₂O) already include their gas-phase corrections. The adsorbate corrections given in Section 2.5 are added to the energy of each adsorbed state. Temperature is 298.15 K, e is the positive elementary charge, and U is an electrode potential.

G ≈ E + ΔZPE − TΔS (S1)

μ(H⁺ + e⁻; U_(RHE)) = ½G(H₂) − eU_(RHE) (S2)

U_(RHE) = U_(SHE) + [k_(B)T ln(10)/e]pH (S3)

At 298.15 K, k_(B)T ln(10)/e = 0.05916 V. Thus pH 13 and 0.78 V versus RHE correspond to approximately 0.011 V versus SHE. Potentials used on an RHE axis receive no additional pH correction.

2.4 Coverage library, stoichiometric accounting and surface maps

Coverage free energies. The library contains 46 supported-model configurations for FeCoMn–NC (43 enumerated configurations plus the joint states 6O* + Mn–O*, 8O* + Mn–O* and 8O* + Mn–OH*, which combine particle O* with an axial ligand on Mn), 24 for FeCo–NC, 18 for Mn–NC, and 44 configurations of an independent FeCo(110) slab (Table S12 and Figure S23). The latter is displayed under both particle-containing samples but retains an independent clean reference. Each candidate is identified by its adsorption composition, site and configuration code in Table S12. Distinct optimized arrangements at the same composition remain distinct candidates. Adsorbate identities (O, OH, OOH and H) were assigned from the optimized structures.

For a candidate relative to its own clean parent, let N_(O) and N_(H) be the total added oxygen and hydrogen atom counts. Let n_(O*), n_(OH*), n_(OOH*) and n_(H*) count the corresponding adsorbed species.

C_(cov) = 0.066n_(O*) + 0.343n_(OH*) + 0.403n_(OOH*) + 0.210n_(H*)  [eV] (S4)

m = 2N_(O) − N_(H) (S5)

G_(form)(0) = E_(state) − E_(clean) − N_(O) G(H₂O) + ½mG(H₂) + C_(cov) (S6)

G_(form)(U_(RHE)) = G_(form)(0) − meU_(RHE) (S7)

G_(form)(U_(SHE, pH)) = G_(form)(0) − meU_(SHE) − mk_(B)T ln(10)pH (S8)

G(U) plots use eV per simulation cell, relative to the clean state of that model family. At a specified potential and pH, the plotted surface map selects the lowest G_(form) only among the candidates of the same family. Crossovers are intersections of those formation-energy lines. Supported-model and independent-slab minima are kept separate.

2.5 Gas-reference sets and adsorbate corrections

Gas references and adsorbate corrections. The coverage curves and Table S12 retain the original coverage-library references G(H₂) = −6.834 eV and G(H₂O) = −14.341 eV. The ORR free energies in Table S8 use the unified references G(H₂) = −6.833 eV and G(H₂O) = −14.327 eV. Total adsorbate corrections ΔZPE − TΔS are +0.066 eV (O*), +0.343 eV (OH*), +0.403 eV (OOH*), and +0.210 eV (H* in the coverage library). The O*, OH* and OOH* values follow Table S1 of the Supporting Information to main-text ref. 12. The H* value is the coverage-library correction.

2.6 ORR backgrounds, balanced steps and adsorption energies

Define B as the surface state under reaction conditions, which is particle 8O* plus axial Mn–OH for FeCoMn–NC, particle 8O* for FeCo–NC, and axial Mn=O for Mn–NC. The background atoms remain part of every state. The added OOH*, O* and OH* bind on the opposite Mn side in the two Mn paths, or on the particle in FeCo–NC. The four balanced acid-form CHE steps are listed below. On the RHE scale, they are thermodynamically equivalent to the alkaline form.

B + O₂ + H⁺ + e⁻ → B–OOH (S9)

B–OOH + H⁺ + e⁻ → B–O + H₂O (S10)

B–O + H⁺ + e⁻ → B–OH (S11)

B–OH + H⁺ + e⁻ → B + H₂O (S12)

ΔG_(O*) = E(B–O) − E(B) − G(H₂O) + G(H₂) + 0.066 eV (S13)

ΔG_(OH*) = E(B–OH) − E(B) − G(H₂O) + ½G(H₂) + 0.343 eV (S14)

ΔG_(OOH*) = E(B–OOH) − E(B) − 2G(H₂O) + ³/₂G(H₂) + 0.403 eV (S15)

G(O₂) + 2G(H₂) − 2G(H₂O) = 4.92 eV (S16)

The last equation fixes the O₂ reference through the equilibrium four-electron water-formation free energy. All adsorption energy differences use the matched relaxation energies in Table S13.

2.7 Free-energy steps, diagrams, limiting potential and overpotential

ΔG₁(0) = ΔG_(OOH*) − 4.92 eV;   ΔG₂(0) = ΔG_(O*) − ΔG_(OOH*) (S17)

ΔG₃(0) = ΔG_(OH*) − ΔG_(O*);   ΔG₄(0) =  − ΔG_(OH*) (S18)

ΔG_(i)(U_(RHE)) = ΔG_(i)(0) + eU_(RHE) (S19)

U_(L) = min_(i)[ − ΔG_(i)(0)/e];   η_(ORR) = 1.23 V − U_(L) (S20)

U_(L) is the highest potential at which all four steps remain exergonic. At 0 V all four ΔG_(i) are negative, and the least negative one sets U_(L); this step is termed the potential-determining step. It follows from the reaction free energies alone, without activation barriers, and therefore does not identify the kinetically slowest step.

Diagrams referenced to the regenerated background plus product water contain the following five levels, in order O₂, OOH*, O*, OH*, H₂O.

(4.92 eV − 4eU, ΔG_(OOH*) − 3eU, ΔG_(O*) − 2eU, ΔG_(OH*) − eU, 0) (S21)

For the hydroxyl-ligated FeCoMn–NC path at 0.78 V, these levels are 1.80, 1.80, 0.67, 0.07 and 0 eV. The OH* structure retains the axial OH and adds the reaction-side OH. The final step is downhill by 0.07 eV and regenerates the same background.

2.8 Scaling relation and volcano plot

Volcano relation. The two-branch activity volcano in Figure 6 is constructed from the same CHE expressions. On the strong-binding side, the OH* → H₂O step limits and eU_(L) = ΔG_(OH*). On the weak-binding side, the O₂ → OOH* step limits and eU_(L) = 4.92 eV − ΔG_(OOH*). The two branches are linked by ΔG_(OOH*) = ΔG_(OH*) + 3.28 eV, where 3.28 eV is the mean ΔG_(OOH*) − ΔG_(OH*) of the three ORR paths (3.30, 3.33, and 3.21 eV for FeCoMn–NC on the 8O* + Mn–OH* background, FeCo–NC, and Mn–NC). The apex therefore lies at ΔG_(OH*) ≈ 0.82 eV with U_(L) ≈ 0.82 V. The three computed paths are shown at their ΔG_(OH*) values (Table S8). The O* level does not enter this two-branch construction. Actual limiting potentials are calculated from the complete four-step paths. In particular, O* → OH* limits the FeCo–NC path. With the commonly used scaling constant of about 3.2 eV,^(5,22) the apex shifts to ΔG_(OH*) ≈ 0.86 eV, and FeCoMn–NC (ΔG_(OH*) = 0.85 eV) lies about 0.01 eV from it.

ΔG_(OOH*) ≈ ΔG_(OH*) + 3.28 eV (S22)

U_(L, volcano) = min[ΔG_(OH*), 4.92 eV − ΔG_(OOH*)]/e (S23)

2.9 Magnetic references

Energy and magnetic references. The FeCo–NC background used for the ORR energies is the relaxed 8O* state, E = −2143.215 eV, with total magnetization approximately 137.5 μB. The electronic descriptors use the corresponding magnetic solution.

2.10 PDOS, contact atoms and occupied d-band centers

Metal d projections and adsorbate-O p projections are selected by retained parent atom identities and the added-adsorbate atom mapping. Spectra are spin-summed, and the occupied d-band center is computed from the unbroadened d-PDOS by eq S24. Site-projected spectra (Figure S27) are plotted in states eV⁻¹ without normalization or smoothing. The orbital-resolved PDOS in Figure S28 is shown as log₁₀(1 + PDOS) with PDOS in states eV⁻¹.

ε_(d) = ∫⁻⁸⁰ E D_(d)(E)dE / ∫⁻⁸⁰ D_(d)(E)dE;   E = ε − E_(F) (S24)

Here, E_(F) is the Fermi level of each state. For multiple contact atoms, the contact d-center is the arithmetic mean of the selected per-atom d-centers. For FeCo–NC, the contact sets are Fe206/Co235 for OOH*, Fe206/Co234 for O*, and Fe206/Co234/Co235 for OH* and the covered background. Element-averaged Fe/Co traces and atom-resolved heat maps include their stated atom sets.

2.11 Bader charge and electron-population accounting

Bader electron populations were obtained with an AECCAR0 + AECCAR2 reference. The contact-metal change is ΔN_(metal) = Σ[N_(metal)(adsorbed) − N_(metal)(background)] over the specified atom set, and negative values denote electron loss. The adsorbate electron gain is the sum of its Bader populations minus the neutral valence-electron count of O, OH, or OOH.

ΔN_(metal) = Σ_(i ∈ contact)[N_(i)(B–X) − N_(i)(B)] (S25)

ΔN_(X) = Σ_(j ∈ X) N_(j)(B–X) − Σ_(j ∈ X) Z_(j, val) (S26)

Magnitude plots use |ΣΔN|, not Σ|ΔN|. For the present C/N/Fe/Co/Mn_(pv)/O/H potentials, the neutral valence count of an O, OH and OOH adsorbate is 6, 7 and 13 electrons, respectively.

2.12 Frozen-fragment charge-density differences and rendering

Charge-density differences are defined by eqs S27 and S28. Positive and negative Δρ denote accumulation and depletion, respectively. The full-cell residuals of the adsorption/interface datasets are below 4.2 × 10⁻⁶ electrons. Neither planar amplitudes nor the origin-dependent cumulative extrema are Bader charges.

Δρ_(ads)(r) = ρ_(B–X)(r) − ρ_(B, frozen)(r) − ρ_(X, frozen)(r) (S27)

Δρ_(int)(r) = ρ_(full)(r) − ρ_(particle, frozen)(r) − ρ_(support, frozen)(r) (S28)

$\Delta\overline{\rho}(z)\  = \ A^{- 1}\ \int_{A}\ \Delta\rho(x,y,z)dxdy$ (S29)

$\Delta N(z)\  = \ A\ \int_{0}^{z}\ \Delta\overline{\rho}(z')dz';\ \ \ {\delta N}_{cell}\  = \ \int_{cell}\ \Delta\rho(r)d^{3}r$ (S30)

A is the in-plane cell area. All three densities in a subtraction share identical cell, PAW settings, atom coordinates for retained atoms and realized FFT dimensions. Charge density is expressed as electrons per Å³. VASP grid values are converted using the cell volume before real-space integration. Positive/negative isosurfaces use ±0.005 e Å⁻³ with accumulation/depletion distinguished in the figure legend. Identical isovalues and local-view scales are used when comparing displayed structures.

The three-dimensional charge-density difference upon OH* adsorption shows electron accumulation around the OH* oxygen and the metal–O bond, with depletion on neighboring atoms. On FeCo–NC, this redistribution involves multiple Fe/Co atoms of the particle (Figure S32).

The planar-averaged charge-density difference at the particle–support interface shows electron depletion above the graphene plane and below the lowest metal layer, with accumulation in the gap between them. FeCoMn–NC and FeCo–NC show similar profiles (Figure S33). The corresponding cumulative charge changes sign with height, reflecting the alternating regions of electron accumulation and depletion near the interface (Figure S34).

2.13 COHP projections and bond-set definitions

COHP analysis. Dedicated VASP wavefunction calculations used ISYM = −1 and NBANDS = 1200 before projection with LOBSTER 5.1.1 and the pbeVaspFit2015 basis, with C/N/O 2s2p, Fe/Co 3d4s, Mn 3p3d4s, and H 1s where present. For the Mn–NC background, the Bader and PDOS analyses used a separate static calculation on the same structure, and the total energies of the two calculations differ by 0.007 eV. The COHP window was −15 to +10 eV and Gaussian smearing was 0.10 eV. Spin-channel absolute charge spillings span approximately 1.04–2.60%. Spectra are spin-summed and shown as −pCOHP, so bonding contributions are positive. −ICOHP is integrated to E_(F). For OH* on FeCo–NC, 3.19 eV is the sum of three metal–O contributions (1.62, 0.74, and 0.82 eV), whereas FeCoMn–NC and Mn–NC each have one Mn–O contact. On FeCoMn–NC, the −ICOHP of the axial Mn–OH bond is 2.64, 3.00 and 2.98 eV in the background, OOH* and OH* states. The axial Mn–OH bond therefore responds to the adsorbate on the opposite side.

 − ICOHP(M–O) =  − ∫^(E_(F)) COHP_(M–O)(E)dE (S31)

Plotted curves use E − E_(F). Contact pairs are selected from the final relaxed geometry, and multiple contacts are explicitly summed only when a Σ label is used.

2.14 Particle binding and removal/recovery analysis

The binding energy compares the supported clean model with the separately optimized matching support and isolated Fe₂₇Co₃₂ cluster. It is reported per 59-atom particle. Negative E_(b) indicates binding relative to those fragments.

E_(b) = E_(supported) − E_(support) − E_(Fe₂₇Co₃₂) (S32)

The recovery test follows clean → seven adsorbed O → removal of those O atoms → re-optimization, retaining the existing selective-dynamics constraints and atom identities. Each case uses one optimized configuration. Periodic displacement vectors are obtained with the minimum-image convention. A rigid translation is removed using the componentwise median displacement of the C/N support atoms, and no arbitrary rotation is fitted to minimize the particle displacement.

δr_(i) = MIC(r_(i, recovered) − r_(i, clean)) − median_(C, N)[MIC(Δr)] (S33)

RMS_(disp) = [N_(M)⁻¹ Σ_(i ∈ Fe, Co) |δr_(i)|²]^(1/2) (S34)

RMS_(pair) = [2Σ_(i < j)(d_(ij, recovered) − d_(ij, clean))² / (N_(M)(N_(M)−1))]^(1/2) (S35)

N_(M) = 59, and the maximum metal displacement is max|δr_(i)|. Pair distances use minimum images. The coordination-count analysis uses a 3.0 Å metal–metal cutoff (self pairs excluded). Mn–N means are taken over the four retained N atoms.

The binding energies of the particle to the support are −3.73 eV in FeCoMn–NC and −4.12 eV in FeCo–NC (Figure S11a and Table S6). After seven O* are adsorbed and then removed, re-optimization restores the metal framework of both particles, with RMS displacements of 0.040 and 0.034 Å from the clean structures (Figures S11b, c and S12 and Table S6).

2.15 Geometry and output acceptance

A usable relaxation must show electronic convergence, ionic convergence and normal termination. Final structures are checked for retention of the particle oxygen background and axial ligand, OOH O–O integrity, actual Mn–O/Mn–N or Fe/Co–O contacts, Mn out-of-plane displacement and total/local moments where available. For Mn–N₄, the local plane is the least-squares plane through the four N atoms, and the height is the Mn displacement projected onto its normal. Dissociated or migrated structures are described by their final geometry.
