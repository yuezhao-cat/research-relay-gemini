## Recommended English abstract

Metal particles frequently coexist with single-atom sites in pyrolyzed metal–nitrogen–carbon (M–N–C) catalysts, but understanding their catalytic behavior requires identifying the surface structures that form under operating conditions. Here, we report an FeCoMn–NC catalyst containing FeCo alloy particles and atomically dispersed Mn sites, achieving a half-wave potential of 0.885 V versus RHE in 0.1 M KOH and retaining 96.5% of its initial current after 238 h. Operando infrared spectroscopy reveals potential-dependent changes in surface species, motivating a thermodynamic analysis of surface coverage under ORR conditions. At 0.78 V versus RHE, the calculations favor O*-covered FeCo particles and axially OH-coordinated Mn sites. Removing the axial OH from the same modeled surface leads to excessively strong intermediate binding, whereas OH coordination produces more favorable ORR energetics. Energy decomposition reveals that this improvement does not arise simply from weakened Mn–adsorbate bonding. Instead, axial OH displaces Mn from the N₄ plane, increasing the structural reorganization energy associated with intermediate adsorption while preserving favorable local electronic interactions. This balance alleviates overly strong OH* adsorption while maintaining nearly thermoneutral OOH* formation at the examined potential. Our findings identify structural reorganization induced by operating-state axial ligation as an important factor governing ORR intermediate binding in particle–single-atom catalysts.

## Precise limitations

- FeCo particles and OH state are co-present in the adopted thermodynamic models; this draft **does not establish particles as the sole physical cause of OH/O site selection**.
- OH* and OOH* energy decomposition differ: for OH* the extra substrate distortion +0.236 eV and frozen interaction +0.100 eV are both unfavorable; for OOH* distortion +0.432 eV is offset by favorable frozen interaction −0.100 eV, plus fragment deformation −0.065 eV. Therefore 'preserving favorable local electronic interactions' is most concrete for OOH*, not necessarily every species.
- A potential issue remains: using 'improvement' may be interpreted as FeCo-caused. It refers here to the matched 8O* FeCoMn surface noOH vs axOH comparison.
- References such as Wan 2022 should be handled accurately in the introduction, not necessarily named in an abstract.
- Title must match the scope: avoid 'FeCo particles determine axial ligand' until the electronic-state inconsistency is resolved.
