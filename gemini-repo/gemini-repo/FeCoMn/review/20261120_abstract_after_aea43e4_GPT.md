## Suggested revised abstract

In metal–nitrogen–carbon (M–N–C) catalysts containing both metal particles and single-atom sites, how the surface structures formed under operating conditions influence oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) intermediate binding remains unclear. Here, we prepare an FeCoMn–NC catalyst in which FeCo alloy particles coexist with Mn single atoms, achieving an ORR half-wave potential of 0.885 V versus RHE. Operando infrared spectroscopy reveals potential-dependent changes in surface species, motivating calculations of surface coverage under ORR conditions. At 0.78 V, the calculations favor O*-covered FeCo particles and Mn single atoms coordinated by an axial OH. ORR intermediates adsorb on the Mn sites opposite this axial ligand. Without the axial OH, Mn binds the intermediates excessively strongly, making OH* removal unfavorable. The axial OH displaces Mn from the N₄ plane, whereas adsorption of OOH* or OH* on the opposite side draws Mn back toward the plane. The resulting structural reorganization increases the energetic cost of adsorption without necessarily weakening the local Mn–adsorbate interaction. Consequently, excessive OH* binding is alleviated, OOH* formation becomes nearly thermoneutral at 0.78 V, and the subsequent ORR steps are downhill. This balance places FeCoMn–NC near the apex of the calculated activity volcano, consistent with its superior ORR performance.
