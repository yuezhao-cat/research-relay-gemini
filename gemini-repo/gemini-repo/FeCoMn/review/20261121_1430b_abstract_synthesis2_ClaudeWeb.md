Metal particles commonly coexist with single-atom sites in pyrolyzed metal–nitrogen–carbon (M–N–C) catalysts, yet catalytic models often rely on bare single-atom surfaces. How the surface state formed under working conditions governs the binding of oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) intermediates remains unclear. Here, we prepare an FeCoMn–NC catalyst in which FeCo alloy particles coexist with Mn single atoms. In 0.1 M KOH, FeCoMn–NC reaches a half-wave potential of 0.885 V versus the reversible hydrogen electrode (RHE), higher than those of commercial Pt/C, FeCo–NC, and Mn–NC, and retains 96.5% of its initial current over 238 h. Operando infrared spectroscopy shows that the interfacial species change with potential, so we calculated the surface coverage under working conditions. At 0.78 V versus RHE, the calculations show O*-covered FeCo particles and adjacent Mn single atoms carrying an axial OH. The axial OH pulls Mn out of the N4 plane, and an ORR intermediate binding on the opposite side draws Mn back toward the plane. This opposing displacement offsets part of the binding energy and moderates the over-strong binding of the ORR intermediates on Mn. Consequently, OH* removal is downhill at 0.78 V, while OOH* formation is still nearly thermoneutral. FeCoMn–NC therefore lies near the apex of the calculated activity volcano, which explains its highest measured activity.
