## Claude 修订稿（243 词）
The oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) activity of single-atom catalysts can be enhanced by neighboring metal particles, but both components are covered by electrolyte species under reaction conditions, and how the particles influence the covered single atoms remains unclear. Here, we show that FeCo alloy particles change the axial ligand of adjacent Mn single atoms under reaction conditions. The new FeCoMn–NC catalyst reaches a half-wave potential of 0.885 V versus RHE in 0.1 M KOH, outperforming commercial Pt/C, FeCo–NC, and Mn–NC, and retains 96.5% of its initial current after 238 h. Operando infrared spectroscopy detects OOH*, which exists only in the associative ORR pathway, and shows that the surface species change with potential. We therefore calculated the surface coverage before the ORR takes place. At 0.78 V versus RHE and pH 13, the FeCo particles are covered by O* and the adjacent Mn single atoms are coordinated by an axial OH, whereas Mn in particle-free Mn–NC is coordinated by an axial O. On these covered surfaces, the strong axial Mn=O bond leaves Mn binding OOH* too weakly, whereas the much weaker axial Mn–OH bond allows Mn to bind OOH* more strongly. FeCo–NC instead binds O* too strongly. FeCoMn–NC therefore binds the ORR intermediates moderately and lies near the apex of the activity volcano, which explains its highest measured activity. The axial OH that FeCo particles bring to the adjacent Mn single atoms thus gives these Mn single atoms moderate binding of the ORR intermediates.
