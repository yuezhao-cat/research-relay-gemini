## 前言最后一段（定稿）
Here, we report an FeCoMn–NC catalyst in which FeCo alloy particles coexist with Mn single atoms. In 0.1 M KOH, FeCoMn–NC reaches a half-wave potential of 0.885 V versus RHE, outperforming commercial Pt/C, FeCo–NC, and Mn–NC. Operando infrared spectroscopy detects OOH*, an intermediate that exists only in the associative ORR pathway, and shows that the species at the catalyst surface change with potential. We therefore calculated the surface coverage of the three catalysts at different potentials and pH values before the ORR takes place. At a representative ORR potential of 0.78 V versus RHE and pH 13, the FeCo particles are covered by O*, and the adjacent Mn single atoms are coordinated by an axial OH. In particle-free Mn–NC, the Mn single atoms are coordinated by an axial O instead. On these covered surfaces, the ORR intermediates bind to the Mn single atoms in FeCoMn–NC and Mn–NC. In Mn–NC, the strong axial Mn=O bond leaves Mn binding the OOH* intermediate too weakly, which hinders OOH* formation. In FeCoMn–NC, the much weaker axial Mn–OH bond allows Mn to bind OOH* more strongly, which facilitates OOH* formation. In FeCo–NC, by contrast, the FeCo particles bind the O* intermediate too strongly, which hinders the conversion of O* to OH*. FeCoMn–NC therefore binds the ORR intermediates moderately and lies near the apex of the activity volcano, which explains why FeCoMn–NC shows the highest measured ORR activity. These results show that FeCo particles cause the adjacent Mn single atoms to be coordinated by an axial OH, and this axial OH gives these Mn single atoms moderate binding of the ORR intermediates.

## 原稿摘要（2240 版）
The local coordination of metal–nitrogen–carbon catalysts governs the binding of oxygen-containing intermediates, which is important for oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) activity. It is necessary to clarify the coordination environment that metal sites adopt under realistic reaction conditions, and how this coordination affects the binding and conversion of ORR intermediates. Here, we prepare an FeCoMn–NC catalyst, in which FeCo alloy particles coexist with dispersed Mn species. The catalyst achieves a half-wave potential of 0.885 V versus the reversible hydrogen electrode (RHE) in 0.1 M KOH and retains 96.5% of its initial current after 238 h. Surface coverage calculations show that the FeCo particle surface is covered by O* and the Mn site carries an axial OH ligand at 0.78 V (vs. RHE) and pH 13. The calculated limiting potential of FeCoMn–NC is 0.78 V, which is much higher than 0.34 V of FeCo–NC and 0.26 V of Mn–NC. Compared with the oxo-ligated Mn site in Mn–NC, FeCoMn–NC forms a stronger Mn–O bond with OOH*, which favors stabilization of the first intermediate. Compared with the FeCo–NC, the FeCoMn–NC shows the weaker binding with OH*, which favors OH* removal in the final step. The axial-hydroxyl-ligated Mn site accommodates both OOH* formation and OH* removal and attains moderate intermediate binding. This work comprehensively investigates the effects of surface coverage, axial coordination and intermediate binding under reaction conditions, and provides a basis for understanding the ORR behavior of catalysts in which particles and dispersed metal sites coexist.

## Claude 版本 A（18:27，强调重要性，237 词）
Placing metal particles near single metal atoms makes M–N–C catalysts highly active for the ORR, an effect commonly attributed to electronic modulation of the single atoms by the particles. Under reaction conditions, however, both the particles and the single atoms are covered by electrolyte species, and how the particles act on the single atoms under this coverage remains unclear. Here, we report an FeCoMn–NC catalyst in which FeCo alloy particles coexist with Mn single atoms. In 0.1 M KOH, FeCoMn–NC reaches a half-wave potential of 0.885 V versus RHE, outperforming commercial Pt/C, FeCo–NC, and Mn–NC, and retains 96.5% of its initial current after 238 h. Surface coverage calculations show that the FeCo particles reverse the preferred axial ligand of the adjacent Mn single atoms: at 0.78 V and pH 13, Mn is coordinated by an axial OH in FeCoMn–NC but by an axial O in particle-free Mn–NC. The strong axial Mn=O bond leaves Mn binding OOH* too weakly, whereas the much weaker axial Mn–OH bond allows Mn to bind OOH* more strongly. Because FeCo–NC binds O* too strongly, only FeCoMn–NC binds the ORR intermediates moderately and reaches a calculated limiting potential of 0.78 V. These findings identify the axial ligand formed under reaction conditions as the link between metal particles and the activity of neighboring single atoms, and show that such catalysts should be understood from their working surfaces rather than their initial structures.

Metal–nitrogen–carbon (M–N–C) catalysts are promising platinum substitutes for the oxygen reduction reaction (ORR), and metal particles adjacent to the single metal atoms can raise their activity, yet how the particles act on the single atoms remains unclear because both components are covered by electrolyte species under reaction conditions. Here we show that FeCo alloy particles reverse the axial ligand preferred by the neighboring Mn single atoms, and that this ligand sets the ORR activity.
